Also fixed — the double jump

You reported that pressing Space once made the Viber jump, but pressing it again in mid-air did nothing.

What was happening: the referee was reading your button press as if you were holding the button down, instead of as a single tap. So the instant you left the ground, it spent your second jump for you — before you had asked for it. By the time you pressed Space again, there was nothing left to use.

The fix: the referee now treats a press as a single event. It happens once, then it is forgotten — exactly how the single-player game already worked. The jump rules themselves were never wrong; only the way the button press was passed to them.

Also caught while fixing it: a press could occasionally be lost completely if it arrived at just the wrong moment. Both problems are solved by the same change.

The same mistake affected dash, both attacks and your special ability — all four are fixed too. And there is now an automatic check that presses Space twice in mid-air and refuses to pass unless the Viber actually double jumps.

2 Why it couldn't work online before

Your game worked like a board game played on one table. Everything — where each fighter is standing, who got hit, who lost a life, who won — was decided inside each player's own browser.

That is perfectly fine for one player. But the moment two browsers play, they each start deciding their own version of the truth. Browser A thinks it hit you. Browser B thinks it dodged. Both are convinced they are right, and there is nobody to settle it.

Think of it like this: two people shouting "I got you!" at each other with no referee. Nothing gets settled, and it looks broken.

3 The fix: one referee in the sky

I put a referee on the internet. It is a small program that runs on Cloudflare's computers — not on your computer, not on your players' computers. There is one referee per match.

Now the browsers behave differently. A browser is only allowed to say one thing: "the player pressed these buttons." It is never allowed to say "I hit him" or "I have three lives." Only the referee decides that, and then tells everybody the same answer.

Why Cloudflare, and not a "VPS"

You said you did not want a VPS — that means renting a computer somewhere and being responsible for keeping it alive. Good instinct.

So I used Cloudflare's own tiny computers instead. Cloudflare runs them for you. There is nothing to keep alive, nothing to update, nothing to restart at 3am. And at your size, it is free.

4 The "never use a robot" rule you asked for

You were very clear: if a real player disconnects, never let a robot take over their character. I want to explain how I handled that, because it is the part I am proudest of.

The obvious way to do it is to write robot code and add a switch that says "do not use the robot." That is fragile — one mistake and the switch flips.

Instead I wrote the referee so that the robot code does not exist at all. There is nothing to switch on. It is not "disabled" — it is absent.

The referee cannot put a robot in a human's seat, because there is no robot in the referee.

So here is what actually happens when someone drops out:

  1. Their character is removed from the arena immediately. It disappears.
  2. Nothing takes its place. Ever.
  3. The remaining humans keep fighting, exactly as before.
  4. If only one human is left, the referee ends the match and declares that person the winner.
  5. The room keeps working even if the person who created it is the one who left.

I tested this by literally closing one of the two browser windows mid-fight. One fighter was left standing, no extra fighter appeared, and the remaining player was declared the winner.

8 Words I used, translated

Every technical word from my earlier messages, in one line each. You never need to use these words yourself — they are here so my other notes are readable.

Server
A program that runs on someone else's computer, on the internet, and that everyone connects to. In your game, this is the referee.
Worker
Cloudflare's name for a small piece of code they run for you. It is the front door — it takes the connection and hands it to the right referee.
Durable Object
Cloudflare's name for one small computer that keeps one thing alive. I use one per room. That is why two people typing the same code always end up in the same match.
WebSocket
A phone line that stays open. Normal web pages ask a question and hang up; a WebSocket stays connected so both sides can talk continuously. That is what makes live movement possible.
Server-authoritative
Fancy way of saying "the referee decides, not the players." It is the single most important idea in this whole project.
Tick
The referee re-checks the whole match 60 times every second. Each check is a "tick."
Snapshot
The referee's report on where everything is, sent to every player 20 times a second. Between reports, the game smoothly fills in the gaps so it looks continuous.
Interpolation
The smooth filling-in mentioned above. It is why the other player glides instead of jumping around.
Prediction
Your own character reacts to your key press instantly, without waiting for the referee to confirm. It feels responsive. If the referee disagrees, your character is nudged back so gently you will not notice.
Room code
The 5-letter password-like code that puts people in the same match. It is also, literally, the name of the referee — that is how everyone finds the same one.
Terminal / command line
The plain black window where you type instructions instead of clicking. You only need it for the one-time internet setup.
npm
The tool that downloads code other people wrote so you do not have to. It comes with Node.js.
Wrangler
Cloudflare's tool that sends your game up to their computers. You only ever type two commands with it.
Deploy
Copying something from your computer onto the internet, so other people can reach it.
Localhost / 127.0.0.1
Computer-speak for "this computer only." Anything at that address works on your machine and nowhere else. Perfect for testing.

9 What is not finished yet

I would rather you hear this from me than discover it with a friend.

LIMIT Dropping out means waiting for the next match

If your internet hiccups and you get disconnected, you can rejoin the room — but you will be watching from the lobby until the current fight ends, rather than being put back into it. I did this on purpose: shoving a player back into a fight in progress is exactly how you end up with two copies of the same person, which is worse than waiting.

LIMIT On slow internet, close calls go the wrong way

If someone has slow internet (roughly 100 milliseconds or worse), sometimes you will both land a hit when you were sure you dodged first. Fixing this properly is a known extra step called "lag compensation." It is the most common next thing to build.

LIMIT I tested with two players, not four

Two players is fully proven. Three and four use the same code and should work, but I have not played a full four-player match, so I am not going to pretend it is verified.

LIMIT You cannot watch a match in progress

Joining a room mid-fight puts you in the lobby rather than as a spectator. Spectating is not built.

LIMIT It has only run on your computer so far

Everything I verified ran on the small server on your machine. Cloudflare's real computers are very close to identical, but not identical. Do the deploy once and play one match — that is the honest last check.
